Graphs

I've been a big fan of Desmos for a while, and my computational geometry class led to these particularly cool graphs.

Line arrangements with depth

Exploring what a line arrangement with depths can look like. Each point is colored by the minimum of its top and bottom depth, where depth is the number of lines above or below it. The context for this was making a Tukey depth oracle for a given set of points.

Minkowski sums via convolution

A Minkowski sum computed with counterclockwise convolution. As far as I know, no other (correct) Desmos implementation of Minkowski sums exists.

Ray-shooting and point-location duality

A demonstration of the duality between polygon ray-shooting queries and point-location queries in the dual. Given a polygon with an identified edge e, you can answer "which edge does a ray from e hit?" by treating every vertex as a segment in ray-space (the one-dimensional locus of ray parameters that hit it), building the trapezoidal decomposition of that ray-space, and querying it. With a history DAG, that runs in O(log n).

Union of disks tangent to the origin

An experimental proof of correctness for an algorithm that computes the union of disks tangent to the origin via the convex hull of their centers. A circular arc appears on the boundary of the union exactly when its center is a hull point. The actual proof involved "inverting" the set of points.

Connected components in a union of disks

An experimental proof of correctness for an algorithm that counts connected components in a union of unit disks: filter the Delaunay triangulation of the centers down to edges of length at most 2r, then count connected components in the resulting graph. The actual proof involved keeping track of Voronoi regions while following a (topological) path in the disk union interior.
